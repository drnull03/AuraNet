For decades, the "castle-and-moat" perimeter has been the backbone of enterprise security, but it remains fundamentally ill-equipped for the modern era of lateral movement, insider threats, and complex microservice architectures. This project introduces **AuraNet**, a revolutionary, autonomous self-healing Zero Trust architecture designed to reclaim digital sovereignty for distributed systems by replacing passive firewalls with a modern, proactive, and truly Zero Trust Mesh.

**AuraNet** achieves its resilient and autonomous nature by building on three foundational pillars of modern cloud-native infrastructure. It utilizes **SPIRE and SPIFFE** as a decentralized identity backbone, ensuring that every workload is cryptographically verified via TPM hardware attestation before communication begins. All network and system-level events are then monitored at the kernel level using **(eBPF with Cilium)**, providing a level of visibility and enforcement speed that is impossible to achieve with traditional user-space tools. Finally, to ensure privacy across departmental boundaries, AuraNet employs **Federated Learning **(Flower Framework with FedProx Algorithm)**, allowing the system to train its global security intelligence without ever exposing sensitive data.

This new paradigm represents a fundamental shift from human-dependent security to **Autonomous Self-Healing**. To achieve this, AuraNet integrates several cutting-edge features. It uses a **Neurosymbolic AI engine** that combines deterministic symbolic logic to immediately neutralize naive threats with a probabilistic **Federated Autoencoder** that learns to identify subtle behavioral anomalies. When a threat is detected, the system triggers an instantaneous "Reflex" block via local eBPF maps before orchestrating a global cluster-wide "Immunization" through automated policy injection and identity revocation.

This report details the architecture and implementation of **AuraNet**, a Kubernetes-native framework deployed via Helm and orchestrated through eBPF-powered DaemonSets. It showcases a practical and powerful vision for the future of secure, private, and self-governing financial infrastructure, reclaiming control over departmental digital sovereignty in an increasingly hostile digital landscape.

This project aimed to follow the guidelines of NIST SP 800-207, which served as the primary reference for implementing a Zero Trust architecture. It also sought to integrate modern technologies and approaches, balancing practical engineering considerations with the goal of maximizing the learning experience for the author.

Towards the end of the project, the author experimented with Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) and later with hybrid cryptographic approaches, following the recommendations of NIST IR 8547 (Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography) and NIST SP 800-227 (Key-Encapsulation Mechanisms). The objective was to explore how the proposed architecture could adapt to the emerging era of quantum computing, which is widely expected to become one of the next major technological shifts following the current wave of artificial intelligence.
